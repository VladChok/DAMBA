# Research notes — BERING / 1990

## Historical anchors

- Late 19th / early 20th centuries: proposals for linking the continents by rail were floated, but no intercontinental rail tunnel was built.
- 1950s: Soviet engineer Pyotr Borisov promoted a Bering Strait pumping dam concept intended to alter Arctic water circulation. It was not an approved construction project or an established climate solution.
- **1 June 1990:** U.S.–Soviet maritime boundary agreement and joint statement supporting a Soviet–U.S. international park around the Bering Strait. None constitutes a construction contract for a dam, railway, or tunnel.
- **1991:** The Soviet Union dissolved.

## Primary documents

- U.S.–Soviet international park joint statement (1 June 1990): https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/soviet-united-states-joint-statement-the-establishment-soviet-united-states-international

## Counterfactual branch

Selected divergence: **1 June 1990**, additional hypothetical diplomatic agreement to commission feasibility studies for a Bering intercontinental crossing. This is not proof that either party seriously considered such a deal in those talks.

Potential partnerships with American banks, Japan, and Europe are plausible subjects of negotiation, not predetermined outcomes. Loan financing creates liabilities; project contracts do not automatically change national institutions or guarantee democratic, financial, or political outcomes.

## Money

Historic money actually spent on Bering dam **construction: no construction took place**. There is no documented, agreed 1990 dam-construction budget in this study.

The $120B displayed on the website is a **scenario assumption**, not an archival number, validated civil-engineering estimate, or historical cost. Its split is illustrative only: rail 65B; crossing 25B; energy 12B; ports and logistics 8B; study 4B; contingency 6B. All in hypothetical circa-1990 USD. Full climate pumping dam costs are excluded.

## Limits

- The political-opportunity ranking is an opinion, not a quantitative historical dataset.
- The scenario curves are symbolic, not GDP, trade, or welfare forecasts.
- Do not conflate sea barriers, climate pumps, railway bridges, and subsea tunnels.
- Do not claim that the Soviet collapse would be averted by a project, that Japan would resolve territorial disputes, or that Western financial institutions would become major Russian lenders automatically.
- Need independent expert review before any numerical environmental impact claims.

## Expanded financial model

The existing six expense categories and three inputs are retained. The total can range from 60 to 300 billion hypothetical circa-1990 USD; foreign share from 0 to 90%; financing horizon from 5 to 30 years. These ranges are author-selected exploration limits, not confidence intervals.

- Domestic contribution = total × (1 − foreign share).
- Foreign capital = total × foreign share.
- Within foreign capital: United States 50%, Japan 30%, Europe 20%.
- At the baseline of 120 / 60% / 15: USSR 48, United States 36, Japan 21.6, Europe 14.4 billion; average total financing requirement 8 billion per year.
- Each expense category scales by total / 120. Category shares remain fixed.
- The financing horizon is not a promised construction duration. Domestic funding denotes the Soviet side at divergence and any successor arrangements later; the UI does not assume the USSR persists until 2026.

This split is invented explicitly as MODEL. No country or bank is claimed to have committed these funds. No interest, inflation, discounting, operating cost, exchange-rate risk, or overruns are included. Moving particles communicate direction only; widths communicate contribution shares. All flows use violet because all are modeled.

## Maps and alternative history

Natural Earth coastline data (public domain) is projected locally with D3 during asset preparation, not loaded as a live map library. The globe uses 1:110m outlines, the local plate 1:50m. Coordinates in the cover caption are approximate geographic orientation. No country boundaries or engineering alignment are claimed.

The two narrative paths have no quantitative vertical axis. The alternative does not assign construction dates to its intermediate steps. Its dependencies include enforceable contracts, debt service, access railways, energy, demand, local and Indigenous participation, environmental review, relations with Japan and Europe, and international governance. Success, failure, and non-completion remain possible. No GDP, investment-return, welfare, or climate-impact numbers are fabricated.

## Visual revision: finance and Russian politics

The cumulative funding chart uses fixed axes of 0–30 years and 0–300 billion hypothetical dollars. At time t within the selected horizon T, total contributions are budget × t / T and foreign contributions are foreign capital × t / T. Lines stop at T. Uniform annual contributions are an explicit model assumption, not a construction schedule. Expense bars remain fixed shares of the scenario budget and are now visible without expanding a disclosure.

Political branches are qualitative author analysis, without scores or probabilities:

- Contracts plus independent arbitration could support more predictable rules; a closed contractor consortium is a possible adverse outcome.
- Regional participation in revenues could increase the Far East's bargaining role; a centrally controlled project could have the opposite effect.
- Mutually beneficial transit could increase incentives for compromise; interstate conflict could still freeze the project.

None of these mechanisms establishes democratization, preservation of the USSR, peace, or a change in specific later events. The three sketches are illustrative possibilities, not exhaustive or equally likely outcomes.

Two factual anchors are independent of these hypotheses: the constitution's adoption on 12 December 1993, verified in the [official MChS publication](https://mchs.gov.ru/dokumenty/bazovye-normativnye-pravovye-akty/federalnye-konstitucionnye-zakony/8357), and creation of the federal-district framework by Decree 849 on 13 May 2000. The [decree's English text is archived at the WTO](https://www.wto.org/english/thewto_e/acc_e/rus_e/wtaccrus58_leg_87.pdf); indexed primary text was available but direct PDF retrieval returned 402. The [Federal Tax Service's institutional history](https://www.nalog.gov.ru/rn27/about_fts/fts/history_fts/history_ufns/) independently confirms the date and original seven districts. The site makes no causal claim linking these events to the absence of a crossing.
